Who it is for

You set it up, for yourself and for your family.

For you

For anyone who walks, works or travels alone. A signal of your own: siren, light, video and audio recording, and the people you chose told with one tap.

It does not call or alert emergency services.

For vulnerable people and their family

Older parents, teenagers, people living alone. Set it up together on a quiet day and pair the family by QR code. When they need anything, one tap tells all of you: their name, their message and, if they choose, where they are.

Not a medical alert device: nobody watches over it and it does not call an ambulance. It needs an iPhone and the internet on both sides. For teenagers: 13 and over, and a recording is not a promise of evidence; laws on recording and consent differ by country, and many schools restrict phones and recording.

If someone comes at you

A sudden siren and a flashing light can startle someone who jumps at you and draw the attention of people nearby, while your people are told and, if you choose, where you are.

It cannot stop an attack or keep anyone safe, and how a person reacts to it is out of my hands. It does not call or alert the police.

More it can do

Recording, Guard and a stop gesture.

Recording

A signal can record sound and video for as long as the alarm runs. With Save to Photos on, videos go to your Photos; the app keeps a small copy with the place and time stamped on it.

Guard

Leaving your phone on a table or on a charger? Press Guard and put it face down. If someone lifts it or pulls the cable, your signal starts.

Stop gesture

Only you stop the alarm: with a gesture you draw across a face on the screen, or with Face ID.

What it cannot do

  • It does not call or alert the police or emergency services.
  • Force-quitting the app or switching the phone off stops it. No app can prevent that.
  • With the phone locked or another app in front, the screen light goes off, video pauses and the guard watches nothing. The sound is meant to keep playing.
  • Push alerts need the internet.
  • Alerts don't sound on a silenced iPhone until Apple allows critical alerts.
  • This is a TestFlight. Some parts are still being tested on real phones; tell me what fails.

Flashing light can trigger seizures in people with photosensitive epilepsy.

Private by design

No sign-up and no ads. Your signals, settings and history stay on your iPhone. Alerts, and recordings a signal sends to your people, pass through my server and are deleted after 7 days.
